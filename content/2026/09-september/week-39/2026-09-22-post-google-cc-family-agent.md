## Hook
Google just gave the family group chat an operator.

## Why this matters
CC shows what a useful consumer agent looks like: one shared identity, selective access to each person's information, memory for the group, and permission before it acts outside that group.

## Mechanism
Up to six adults can share specific emails, calendars, Drive files, photos, or Chat messages with CC. It runs on an isolated cloud computer using Gemini and Google's Antigravity agent harness, then turns that context into a daily brief, shared calendar updates, task lists, documents, and pre-filled forms.

## Proof/use case
Google's example is a school event: share the email or a photo of the schedule, let CC add the date and task, then include the update in everyone's morning brief. Google says CC only sees what members choose to share and requires permission before sharing information or acting outside the group.

## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- Call CC an experimental Google Labs product, not a generally available Google service.
- Availability is limited to adults in the U.S. with personal Google accounts; new users join a waitlist.
- Say CC can pre-fill forms with permission, not that it can autonomously sign or submit them.
- The permission, privacy, and isolation descriptions are Google's product claims; they are not an independent security audit.
- Avoid implying CC sees an entire account: each member chooses which information to share.
