## Hook
Deel says one internal AI system replaced 600 people's worth of repetitive work.

## Why this matters
The useful lesson is not “replace your team.” It is that operations agents become valuable when domain experts can teach them real workflows, review the logic, and keep approvals around sensitive actions.

## Mechanism
Akai records a person completing a workflow—including clicks, spoken context, and server calls—then maps the steps, builds connectors, and proposes rules and guardrails. A human reviews the workflow before agents run it on a schedule.

## Proof/use case
Deel says it built more than 8,000 agents representing roughly 600 full-time employees' workload. In payment reconciliation, the company claims Akai automated 85% of the process and removed more than 500 manual hours per week.

## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- Attribute the 8,000-agent, 600-FTE, 85%, 500-hours, and revenue figures to Deel; they are company-reported and not independently audited here.
- Do not say Akai eliminated 600 jobs. Deel describes the figure as equivalent workload and says headcount did not increase during a period of growth.
- Do not call Akai open source. Deel launched it as an external commercial product.
- Do not imply every workflow can be safely automated; financial, HR, and compliance actions require permissions, review, audit trails, and human escalation.
- Avoid presenting screen recording alone as the mechanism; Deel says Akai also captures spoken context and server calls.
