## Hook
Google just made AI search visibility measurable and optional, which is a much bigger deal than another vague AI search update.

## Why this matters
For creators, solo builders, and operators, the frustrating part of AI search has been simple: your content could influence AI answers, but you had almost no control over that distribution and almost no reporting on what was happening. Google's August 31 update changes that. Website owners can now choose whether their links and content appear in generative AI Search features, and Search Console is getting appearance and impression insights for those surfaces. That turns AI search from a black box into something closer to a real channel you can measure, test, and decide how much you want to participate in.

## Mechanism
Google updated its website-owner controls on August 31, 2026 with a Search Console toggle that lets sites decide whether they want to appear in and help ground responses in AI Overviews, AI Mode, and AI Overviews in Discover. Google says opting out removes traffic and impressions from those generative AI surfaces, but the setting is not used as a ranking signal for normal search results outside those features. The company also says Search Console is rolling out generative AI insights that show impressions, which pages appear in AI responses, and in which countries those appearances happen. Search Engine Land reported the rollout is going global after earlier testing, which matters because this is no longer just a limited experiment for a handful of site owners.

## Proof/use case
The proof is not only the new toggle. It is the scale attached to it. In the same August 31 update, Google said AI Overviews now has more than 2.5 billion monthly active users and AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly users. That means this is already large enough to affect discovery strategy, not just future-proofing. For Olufemi's audience, the immediate use case is straightforward: if you run a content site, docs site, or product site, you can now track whether AI search is surfacing your pages, compare that against click-through behavior, and make a deliberate call on whether AI visibility helps your business model or weakens it.
