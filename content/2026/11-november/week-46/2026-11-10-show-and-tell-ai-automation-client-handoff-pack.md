## Audience + difficulty

Freelancers, automation consultants, agencies, founders, and small teams that build AI workflows for clients or internal operations. Intermediate: the viewer needs one approved process, a workflow builder such as n8n or Make, and a place to store the handoff document.

## Why now (1 sentence)

AI automation tutorials keep attracting large audiences, but the useful deliverable after the demo is a client-ready handoff pack that explains what runs, who owns it, how to test it, and when a person must stop it.

## 3 hook options (<12 words each)

## Final record-ready script

Here is the finished result: one client handoff pack with the workflow map, field definitions, test cases, runbook, owner, and stop conditions on screen.

Start with one approved process brief and a sample input. Build or inspect the automation in n8n, Make, or your usual workflow tool. Then give the AI assistant the process brief, the workflow export, and the expected output. Ask it to produce four things: a plain-English map of each step, a field dictionary showing where data comes from and where it goes, a test table with passing and failing cases, and a short runbook for the person who owns the workflow.

Do not accept the document as truth just because it is neatly written. Compare every step with the actual workflow. Run a clean sample, a missing-data sample, and one failure case. Correct the fields, credentials, assumptions, and error path that do not match.

The final page names the owner, the trigger, the systems touched, the logs to check, and the exact condition that stops the run. A client can understand what they are receiving, and your team has something to use when the workflow breaks.

Keep deployment and real client data behind human approval. Export the reviewed version, store it beside the workflow, and record the date of the last test.
