## Audience + difficulty

- **Audience:** freelance writers, content agencies, founders, and small marketing teams that turn briefs into articles and need a reviewable evidence trail before delivery.
- **Difficulty:** intermediate; the operator needs an approved brief, a source pack or browsing-capable AI tool, a document editor, and enough subject knowledge to check claims.
- **Tools:** a redacted client brief, ChatGPT Search or another research tool, a spreadsheet or table for the claim ledger, an AI drafting tool, a document editor, and a timer.
- **Input:** one bounded article brief with reader, purpose, length, required points, excluded claims, deadline, source permissions, and revision limit.
- **Output:** one article draft, one claim ledger with source URLs and supporting passages, one short handoff note, and one time log.
- **Finishing detail:** mark unresolved claims in the working draft, open the original source for every material fact, and label the package for client review rather than treating the AI draft as approved copy.
- **Trust boundary:** AI can organize research and draft from supplied evidence. The writer checks scope, sources, calculations, permissions, tone, and final wording before delivery.
- **Use cases:** freelance blog articles, agency client content packages, founder comparison or help articles, and creator scripts built from approved research.
- **Demand gate:** passed at medium demand with clear buyer intent and a highly demonstrable result. Two specific YouTube tutorials teach adjacent versions of the workflow; Reddit and current public-web workflow notes corroborate the claim-ledger and human fact-checking need. The signals do not prove savings, quality, or earnings.
- **Duplicate gate:** passed on 2026-11-24. Recent content covers book-grounded research, a research-to-client deck, recording-to-content, client scoping, and content repurposing. This workflow has a different input, output, and trust object: a client article backed by a claim-by-claim evidence ledger, not a research notebook, deck, transcript pipeline, or scope document.

## Why now (1 sentence)

Recent tutorials and public writing workflows keep converging on the same practical problem: an AI draft can sound finished while its links, numbers, dates, and scope do not support the sentences beside them.

## Final record-ready script

A polished article can still be a bad client deliverable.

The problem is usually not the first draft.

It is the missing evidence trail behind the draft.

This workflow turns one client brief into an article, a claim ledger, and a review note.

Start with a redacted brief.

Include the reader, the decision they need to make, the required points, the excluded claims, the word range, and the revision limit.

Ask the AI to turn that brief into a checklist.

It now knows what the article must cover and what it must not invent.

That matters because a vague brief gives you a vague draft.

Next, make a claim ledger before you write.

Use four columns: claim, original source, supporting passage, and date checked.

Give the research tool three or four specific questions.

Ask it to separate facts from suggestions and mark anything it cannot verify as unresolved.

Treat every returned link as a lead.

Open the original page yourself.

Check that the passage supports the exact sentence you plan to write.

Now ask the AI for an outline using only the approved brief and checked claims.

Draft one section at a time.

Tell it to use only the verified facts and write verification needed wherever evidence is missing.

The result should be useful prose without quietly turning a guess into a fact.

Run three separate reviews.

First, compare every factual sentence with the ledger.

Second, edit for the reader.

Third, check the agreed scope, links, tone, word count, and revision boundary.

Then record the whole job in a simple time log.

Track briefing, research, drafting, edits, delivery, and revisions.

That tells you whether the project was profitable instead of assuming faster drafting means higher profit.

Deliver the article with the ledger and a short handoff note.

Keep unresolved claims visible.

Ask for client review before publication.

AI can help organize the work and produce a first pass.

You still own the source check and the decision to send it.

Comment LEDGER for the claim-table template.
