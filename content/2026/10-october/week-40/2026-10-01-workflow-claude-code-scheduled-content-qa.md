## Final record-ready script
Your content backlog does not need another writer. It needs a weekly QA operator.

A small content team can spend an hour checking frontmatter, repeated topics, broken source links, unsupported claims, and draft status. That work is repetitive, but the final judgment still belongs to a human.

Use Claude Code scheduled tasks to inspect the repository on a recurring schedule. The agent reads your project rules and recent content, checks the structure and likely duplicates, and produces an evidence-first QA report.

Then it prepares the smallest proposed patch or pull request. You review the diff, source links, and uncertain claims before anything is merged or published.

The workflow is simple: schedule the inspection, make the report visible, keep the proposed changes reviewable, and reserve the consequential decision for a person.

Track the minutes spent on routine QA and how many proposals are accepted without meaning-changing edits. The goal is not autonomous publishing. The goal is faster, safer review.

## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- Do not claim scheduled Claude Code runs guarantee publish-ready content; frame the output as a QA report and proposed diff.
- Do not imply hooks or validators prove content truth; they support structure and deterministic checks.
- Keep merge, publish, and status changes under human approval.
- Treat time savings and accepted-proposal rate as metrics to measure over four runs, not guaranteed results.
