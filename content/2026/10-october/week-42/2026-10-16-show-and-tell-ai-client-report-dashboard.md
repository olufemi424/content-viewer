## Final record-ready script
This is the final result: a branded client report with a KPI summary, two charts, plain-English insights, and a next-step recommendation, generated from one raw data export. Step one, use a redacted CSV or Google Sheet with a date range, metric definitions, and the client’s reporting goals. Step two, clean the columns and calculate the agreed KPIs in a spreadsheet or n8n. Step three, give the AI the verified table and ask it to write only evidence-backed observations, with a source row beside every claim. Step four, populate a reusable Google Docs, Slides, or PDF template. Step five, review the numbers, tone, caveats, and recommendations, then approve the report. The finishing detail is an evidence strip that links each insight to a metric and date range, so the client can see what changed and why. Use this for marketing performance, project health, campaign updates, or weekly operations. The AI drafts; the owner remains responsible for the numbers and the send. ## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- Say the workflow can draft or populate a report; do not promise accurate analysis without validating the source data, metric definitions, and calculations.
- Treat all view and like counts as observations recorded on 2026-10-16; YouTube metrics change over time and may differ by viewer or region.
- Do not claim the workflow saves a specific number of hours or improves client retention; the cited videos make productivity or outcome claims that are not independently validated here.
- Use fictional or redacted data in the recording. Do not expose client identifiers, spend, revenue, or confidential campaign details.
- Keep recommendations labeled as suggestions, especially for financial, health, legal, or other high-stakes decisions.
- Require human approval before sending, publishing, changing a client-facing dashboard, or acting on a recommendation.
