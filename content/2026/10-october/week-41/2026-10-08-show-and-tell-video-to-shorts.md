## Final Result
A visible, ready-to-post content pack made from one long recording: 10+ vertical short-video candidates with burned-in captions, titles/hooks, descriptions, and platform-ready exports. The demo’s final screen shows a grid of the approved shorts, with one playing in a vertical preview and each file labeled with its source timestamp.

**Exact input:** one podcast, webinar, interview, tutorial, livestream, or founder video plus its transcript.

**Tool/action:** upload the recording to an AI clipping tool such as OpusClip, review the suggested moments, reframe/caption the winners, and export the approved clips. A transcript/LLM pass can be used alongside it to generate titles, descriptions, and a repurposing checklist.

**Output:** 10+ candidate clips, a shortlist of approved clips, and a small metadata pack for posting.

**First-three-seconds visual:** open on the completed vertical-video grid and play the strongest clip before showing the upload step.

## Why People Want This
One useful long-form recording often creates a second job: finding the strongest moments, cutting them into vertical video, adding readable captions, writing hooks, and keeping track of what is ready to publish. Creators and small teams want the outcome—not another AI tool tour—because the output is immediately understandable and reusable across several channels.

The workflow also has a clear business use: a freelancer or agency can turn a client’s recurring podcast, webinar, or founder recording into a repeatable monthly deliverable. The human review step matters because an AI-selected clip can omit context, misread a speaker, or produce a weak opening.

## Tools and Inputs

- **Source recording:** MP4/MOV podcast, interview, webinar, tutorial, or livestream; ideally with clear speech and a transcript.
- **AI clipper:** OpusClip or a comparable tool that detects highlights, reframes to vertical, and generates captions. Tool choice is swappable; the result is the teaching unit.
- **Transcript/LLM pass:** use the transcript to label topics, check quotes, and draft title/description variants. Keep the source timestamp beside each candidate.
- **Review surface:** a folder, board, or spreadsheet with `candidate`, `needs-context`, `approved`, and `rejected` states.
- **Export settings:** 9:16 vertical, readable captions, safe margins, source filename, and timestamp in the review record.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

1. **Choose one source recording.** Pick a video with a clear promise or a useful answer. Download or locate the transcript and keep the original file unchanged.
2. **Upload the source to the clipper.** Import the video, choose vertical/short-form output, and let the tool identify candidate moments. The visible output at this point is a set of rough clip suggestions, not a publish-ready result.
3. **Create the candidate list.** Record each suggested clip’s timestamp, working hook, topic, and context warning. Ask an LLM to label the transcript moments as useful, self-contained, on-brand, or missing context; do not let it invent quotes.
4. **Reject weak moments early.** Remove clips that start mid-sentence, depend on a visual that is not included, repeat another candidate, or make a claim the source does not support. Keep the best 10–15 candidates for editing.
5. **Reframe and caption.** Apply a 9:16 crop, check the speaker’s face/subject is visible, and use captions with high contrast and safe margins. Correct names, numbers, and technical terms manually.
6. **Add the metadata pack.** For each surviving clip, write two or three hook/title options, a short description, a platform-appropriate caption, and the source timestamp. The metadata is derived from the clip; it is not a replacement for checking the clip.
7. **Review and approve.** Watch every candidate at normal speed. Approve only clips that are self-contained, accurate, legible, and useful without the long video open. Mark the rest rejected or needs-context.
8. **Export the ready-to-post pack.** Export the approved vertical videos and save the metadata alongside them. Use a stable naming convention such as `topic_03_01m42s_approved.mp4`.
9. **Show the before/after.** Return to the final grid: one long source recording on the left, 10+ approved shorts and their metadata on the right. This is the payoff shot.

## Finishing Detail
Do not publish the first AI cut. Add a **source-timestamp and context check** to every clip, then fix the first one or two seconds so the viewer immediately knows the promise. The highest-value finishing pass is usually a human edit to the opening sentence, caption timing, and crop—not another prompt.

In the screen demo, zoom into one clip’s review record showing: source timestamp, corrected caption, hook, `approved` status, and export filename. That small traceability detail makes the workflow credible for client work and prevents a polished but contextless clip from shipping.
