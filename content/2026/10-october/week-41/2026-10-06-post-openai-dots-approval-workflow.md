## Final record-ready script
Your always-on AI needs a job description, not blanket permission.

OpenAI just introduced Dots: persistent agents with their own cloud computer, browser, and connections to more than four thousand apps. They can keep projects moving in the background and bring decisions back to you.

The useful part is not “AI that works 24/7.” It is the control model. Background research is read-only. You choose connected apps. Custom Rules can allow, block, or require approval for specific actions. And sensitive work can stay with the human.

OpenAI gives one example: a Dot noticed a missing publication invoice, prepared it, then sent it only after approval.

That is the workflow to copy. Give your agent one recurring responsibility, read-only access by default, and a clear approval gate before it sends, publishes, pays, deletes, or changes records. Review its activity weekly, then expand permissions only when the evidence earns it.

Save this before you deploy an always-on agent.

## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- Describe Dots as rolling out to eligible Pro and Business Premium users, with an Enterprise beta controlled by workspace admins; do not imply universal availability.
- Attribute the invoice example to OpenAI's early testing rather than presenting it as independently verified performance.
- Do not imply that approval controls eliminate mistakes or prompt-injection risk; OpenAI explicitly says Dots can still make mistakes.
- Treat the recommended one-responsibility setup and weekly review as operational guidance, not product requirements.
- Avoid calling every connected app fully actionable; permissions and available tools vary by app and plan.
