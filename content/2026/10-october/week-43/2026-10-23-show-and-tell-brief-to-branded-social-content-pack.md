## Audience + difficulty
- **Audience:** creators, freelance social-media managers, founders, agencies, and small marketing teams.
- **Difficulty:** beginner-to-intermediate.
- **Exact input:** one product or campaign brief, a brand voice note, approved colors/fonts, logo assets, audience, offer, and 30-day publishing constraints.
- **Tools:** Claude for the content matrix and draft copy; Canva for branded layouts and batch design; a spreadsheet or Notion table for the approval queue.
- **Output:** a 30-day content calendar plus a named, branded set of social graphics with captions, platform, publish date, status, and one human-approved template.
- **Finishing detail:** add a small evidence/status strip to each row: source claim, CTA, approval state, and `NEEDS REVIEW` when the brief does not support a promise.

## Why now (1 sentence)
Multiple high-view tutorials show people actively want the concrete Claude-plus-Canva outcome of turning a brief into dozens of usable social designs, while fast-growing open-source social-content tools show the workflow has repeatable buyer intent.

## 3 hook options (<12 words each)

## Final record-ready script
This is the finished result: one product brief turned into a 30-day branded content calendar, a batch of ready-to-review social graphics, and captions that still sound like the business. Step one: I give Claude the offer, audience, approved claims, brand voice, colors, fonts, and publishing constraints, not a vague request to “make content.” Step two: Claude turns that input into a matrix with dates, platforms, content angles, captions, visual direction, calls to action, and a status for every post. Step three: I move the approved content into Canva, apply one locked brand template, and batch-create the graphics instead of designing each card from zero. Step four: I review every claim, date, link, and CTA, and mark anything unsupported as NEEDS REVIEW. The output is a calendar and a named content pack the creator, freelancer, agency, or small team can actually schedule. Follow for more creative AI workflows.

- Use a fictional or redacted brief for the recording. Do not expose client strategy, private brand assets, personal information, or unpublished offers.
