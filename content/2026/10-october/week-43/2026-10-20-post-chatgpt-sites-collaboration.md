## Final record-ready script
Your prompt-built app can finally become a shared team tool.

ChatGPT Sites now lets you invite teammates to edit, save, and publish a Site. You can also share one privately with specific people, inspect its database, and connect a custom domain where that option is available.

That matters because generating a page was never the hardest part. The real test is whether a client portal, launch tracker, or onboarding hub can survive handoffs and repeated updates.

Here is a practical first use case: build a private client portal with milestones, approved files, and next steps. Invite one trusted collaborator as an editor, test every link and permission, then publish the first version yourself.

One caution: editors can inspect the live database and publish later versions after the owner's first publish. Give edit access only to people you trust, and review sensitive data before sharing.

Save this for your next lightweight internal tool.

## Risk check (claims needing cautious phrasing)
- OpenAI says more than five million Sites have been created; this is vendor-reported and is not needed to support the workflow recommendation.
- Custom-domain and collaboration availability can vary by plan, account, workspace configuration, and rollout status.
- Editors can inspect the live database and, after the owner's first publish, publish later versions; they should be trusted and access should be reviewed.
- Publishing can affect a Site's database before a new version goes live, and restoring code does not restore database contents or uploaded files.
- A prompt-built Site is appropriate for a focused website or lightweight app; more complex products may still need a separate engineering effort.
